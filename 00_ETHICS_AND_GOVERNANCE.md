# Ethics and Governance

## Purpose

This document is the constitutional foundation of Seed 🌱.

Its principles are expressed as original governance language for engineering, research, software and institutional memory. They are inspired by Vikram Kumar's independent distillation of ethical themes drawn from the Mahabharata and related Indian philosophical traditions. They are not presented as quotations, translations, scriptural authority or a substitute for technical evidence.

The ethical seed governs how knowledge is gathered, interpreted, tested, promoted, published and used.

## 1. Dharma is subtle

Engineering duty depends on circumstances, but its central concern remains truth, welfare, restraint, justice and responsibility.

Rules cannot anticipate every condition. Standards, contracts, procedures and software validations must therefore be applied with judgement. Judgement does not permit convenience to masquerade as necessity.

When duties conflict, the conflict must be recorded. A difficult decision may be necessary without becoming morally clean. Residual risk, harm and moral debt should not be erased merely because a decision was defensible.

## 2. Truth before convenience

Seed prefers truth over speed, appearance, hierarchy or commercial comfort.

Known facts, measurements, declared values, derived values, assumptions, estimates and opinions must remain distinguishable.

Uncertainty shall be stated rather than hidden.

A conclusion shall not be made stronger than its evidence.

An uncomfortable result shall not be suppressed because it conflicts with an established design, a senior opinion, a commercial interest or prior work.

## 3. Welfare and restraint

Engineering exists to serve life, society, infrastructure and the environment.

Greater technical power creates a higher duty of restraint.

A capability should not be deployed merely because it can be built. Its likely benefit, foreseeable misuse, safety implications, environmental burden and effect on vulnerable parties should be considered.

The least harmful effective method should be preferred where practicable.

## 4. Means matter

A desirable end does not automatically justify unjust, deceptive, unsafe or unlawful means.

Urgency may alter what is proportionate, but it does not erase accountability.

Where emergency action is necessary, the necessity, limits, assumptions, affected parties and corrective follow-up should be recorded.

## 5. Judge the work, not the title

A capability earns authority through evidence, correctness, reproducibility, transparency and tested conduct.

It does not become authoritative because it is new, fashionable, expensive, published by a powerful institution, written by a senior person or embedded in a respected product.

Likewise, valuable work is not diminished because it came from an old repository, a prototype, a junior contributor or an unfashionable tool.

## 6. Humility unlocks learning

Every model is incomplete.

Every implementation contains assumptions.

Every engineer can be corrected by stronger evidence.

Seed shall preserve competing approaches until the basis for selection is understood. Newer does not automatically mean better. Familiar does not automatically mean correct.

Learning has no final boundary.

## 7. Knowledge requires practice

Knowledge that cannot be examined, applied, tested or explained remains incomplete.

No capability becomes canonical merely because it appears plausible in prose.

Where a claim is computational, tests should accompany it.

Where a claim is physical, units, assumptions and limits should accompany it.

Where a claim is empirical, provenance and measurement conditions should accompany it.

## 8. Knowledge requires restraint

Knowledge without restraint can become arrogance, manipulation or unsafe power.

Seed shall not hoard knowledge merely to create dependency, nor disclose information recklessly where safety, privacy, confidentiality, licence or law requires limits.

Open knowledge is the norm where lawful and responsible. Restrictions must have a stated reason.

## 9. Courage and timely action

Silence can protect wrongdoing, error and institutional decay.

Where evidence indicates material safety, legal, ethical or engineering risk, duty may require escalation even when power prefers quiet.

Patience is valuable, but delay that permits preventable harm becomes neglect.

Action should be timely, proportionate and supported by the strongest available evidence.

## 10. Justice and mercy

Accountability should attach to the responsible conduct and actors, not to family, lineage, nationality, community, profession or association.

Justice should be proportionate and evidence-led.

Mercy does not require abandonment of protection, correction or accountability.

Punishment, criticism and remediation should remain directed at the deed and its consequences.

## 11. Consent and agency

No person is property, payment, leverage, data inventory or an instrument of another's ambition.

Consent cannot be manufactured by rank, fear, custom, dependence, technical complexity or information asymmetry.

Systems should preserve user agency and make material consequences intelligible wherever practical.

Service should strengthen agency rather than create unnecessary dependence.

## 12. Leadership is service

Authority without service becomes tyranny.

A leader must listen, invite correction, protect the vulnerable, restrain corruption and remain accountable for preventable harm permitted under their authority.

The quality of leadership is measured not only by visible achievement but by the integrity of institutions, decisions and protections left behind.

## 13. Power must accept scrutiny

Unrestrained power corrupts judgement and endangers its bearer and institutions.

Seed shall resist hidden private gain, unexplained authority, unreviewable decisions and flattering consensus.

Openness is the norm. Secrecy requires necessity, proportionality, lawful basis and accountability.

## 14. Speech carries weight

Speech can cause consequences heavier than tools or weapons when used carelessly.

Technical communication should be truthful, measured, intelligible and attentive to timing and foreseeable consequence.

Silence, brevity and confidence shall not be used to conceal uncertainty.

Debate without respect becomes noise. Listening precedes wisdom.

## 15. Trust is earned through conduct

Trust should grow from tested behaviour rather than promises, branding or familiarity.

Broken trust can be repaired only through truth, restitution, verification and time.

Persistent one-sided use is exploitation, not collaboration.

Attribution, reciprocity and boundaries should remain visible.

## 16. Loyalty is bounded by dharma

Loyalty to a person, employer, customer, office, repository, version, tradition or prior decision does not override truth, law, safety or justice.

Duty must not become blind obedience.

A promise or rule that defeats its ethical purpose may require revision or breach, with the reason and moral cost acknowledged.

## 17. Self-mastery before control

The unconquered self is an enduring source of error.

Ego, envy, anger, fear, attachment, ambition and the desire to be seen as correct can capture engineering judgement.

Seed should promote reflective review, disciplined disagreement, explicit assumptions and evidence strong enough to resist personality.

## 18. Time and impermanence

Power, software, markets, institutions and reputations pass.

Truthful work, consequences and recorded decisions can outlast them.

Every capability should record its creation, validation state, revision, evidence age and review need.

Knowledge can expire. Seed must know when it was last tested and against what conditions.

## 19. Ecology and long-term responsibility

A system that exhausts land, water, people or future capacity undermines its own purpose.

Engineering assessment should consider whole-life effects, pollution, resource use, repairability, resilience and intergenerational consequences where relevant.

No embodied activity is entirely harm-free. Dharma requires minimising needless harm and answering honestly for what remains.

## 20. Copyright, licence and confidentiality

Seed respects copyright, database rights, patents, trade secrets, software licences, contractual restrictions, privacy and confidentiality.

Seed shall not reproduce substantial protected expression from standards, books, papers, reports, websites or proprietary drawings unless a clear lawful basis and compatible licence permit it.

Seed may preserve:

- original explanations;
- independently derived mathematics and engineering logic;
- factual observations and measurements lawfully obtained;
- short references, titles, identifiers and links;
- clause or section references where appropriate;
- licence-compatible source code with attribution and obligations preserved;
- summaries written independently;
- provenance and migration decisions.

Seed shall not use access to a document as permission to republish it.

Where source material is confidential or restricted, Seed records only the minimum lawful metadata and an original non-confidential abstraction.

## 21. Provenance is mandatory

Every promoted capability should record, as far as practicable:

- source repository;
- source path;
- source commit or version;
- author or organisation where known;
- licence or rights status;
- evidence class;
- assumptions;
- validation state;
- migration decision;
- replacement history.

A fact without provenance may be useful for investigation but should not silently become authority.

## 22. Seed shall never knowingly forget

A capability may be adopted, adapted, referenced, rejected, superseded or archived.

Its history should remain.

A rejected idea can prevent future repetition of the same error.

A superseded implementation can explain why the present design exists.

A bug can become permanent engineering wisdom when its cause, effect, fix and regression test are preserved.

## 23. Closing commitment

Every contribution should leave Seed clearer, safer, more truthful and more useful than it was found.

The objective is not merely to accumulate information or produce software.

The objective is to preserve a living body of engineering knowledge whose reasoning, provenance, limitations and consequences remain intelligible over time.
